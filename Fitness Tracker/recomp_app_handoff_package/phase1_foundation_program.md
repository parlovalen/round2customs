## 2. Nutrition Targets

**Estimated maintenance calories (TDEE):** ~2,750–2,900 kcal/day

*Method:* Mifflin-St Jeor BMR (10 × 84.8kg + 6.25 × 180.3cm − 5 × 42 + 5 ≈ 1,770 kcal), scaled by an activity multiplier of ~1.6 to account for a desk job plus 4 lifting sessions and 2–3 soccer sessions per week. This is a starting estimate, not a fixed number — real-world TDEE varies by up to 10–15%, so we'll correct it using your actual weight trend over the first 2–3 weeks rather than trusting the formula blindly.

**Starting daily target: 2,600 kcal** (a modest ~200–300 kcal below the TDEE estimate, small enough to preserve training performance while nudging fat loss)

**Adjustment rule (apply every 2 weeks):**
- Weight trending down ~0.3–0.6%/week *and* lifts progressing → target is working, keep it
- Weight stable, but waist measurement shrinking and strength climbing → also working (this is recomp working exactly as intended — don't panic about the scale)
- Weight dropping faster than ~0.7%/week, or strength stalling/reversing → add 150–200 kcal (mostly carbs)
- Weight not moving at all after 3 weeks and measurements unchanged → cut 150–200 kcal

## 3. Weekly Training Schedule (Template)

You have 4 lifting days plus 2–3 soccer sessions — that's up to 7 activity days/week, so *sequencing matters more than the exact calendar days*, especially around your knees.

**Guiding rules:**
1. Never put your two Lower-body lifting days back-to-back — leave at least 48 hours between them.
2. Where possible, shift a Lower-body lifting day so it isn't within ~24 hours of a soccer match (soccer already loads the knees heavily via cutting/deceleration).
3. If a soccer match happens the day before a scheduled Lower day and your knee feels irritated, swap that day for the Upper session instead and push Lower back a day. Flexibility beats rigid adherence here.

## 4. Phase 1: Foundation (Weeks 1–4)

### Goals
- Learn correct movement patterns on all main lifts before adding meaningful load
- Build baseline tolerance for training volume without excessive soreness
- Begin a daily/near-daily tendon-loading routine for the knee (see Section 5) — this matters more than any single lift in this phase
- Establish the habit of showing up 4x/week, logging every session
- No lift should feel maxed out — target RPE 6–7 (leaving 3–4 reps in reserve) throughout

### Progression rule for Weeks 1–4
- **Week 1:** Learn the movements. Use light-to-moderate load. RPE 6.
- **Week 2:** Same exercises, same reps. If Week 1 felt clean (good form, no knee pain >2/10), add ~5% load on main lifts. RPE 6–7.
- **Week 3:** Repeat the Week 2 progression logic. RPE 7.
- **Week 4:** Light/assessment week — reduce sets by ~1 per exercise, keep load, focus on movement quality. Re-test how the knee is responding before Phase 2 adds more volume.

**General overload logic used throughout the program (not just Phase 1):** if you hit the top of the rep range for all sets at the target RPE, add weight next session. If you miss reps or RPE creeps above 8, repeat the same weight. If sleep, soreness, or soccer load was unusually high, it's fine to repeat a session rather than push forward — consistency beats forcing progression on a bad day.

## 5. Patellar Tendon Care Protocol (do this in addition to the lifting days)

Current evidence on patellar tendinopathy (jumper's knee) supports **isometric and heavy-slow-resistance loading** as the most effective non-surgical approach — you're already getting this from the Spanish squat holds and box squats above, but the extra piece that helps most is *frequency*:

- **Daily or every-other-day:** Spanish squat or wall sit isometric hold, 4-5 x 30-45s, at an intensity where mild discomfort (≤3/10) is acceptable but pain shouldn't increase afterward or the next day.
- **Ankle mobility** (the dorsiflexion rocks in the warm-ups) is included deliberately — restricted ankle mobility is associated with increased knee stress during squatting movements.
- Avoid deep lunges, jump training, and full-depth loaded squats for now. These can be reintroduced gradually in later phases once the tendon tolerates current loading well.
- If pain is worse the morning after training (not just during), that's the clearest signal to back off load or depth and consider a physical therapy consult.

## 6. Sleep & Habits — Month 1 Focus

Your 7-hour window (midnight–7 AM) is on the lower edge of the commonly recommended 7–9 hours for adults, but a **fixed, consistent schedule** is itself valuable for recovery and hormone regulation — don't discount that. If you ever have flexibility to extend it toward 7.5–8 hours, that's a worthwhile experiment later, but it's not the priority right now.

**Pick 2 habits to focus on for Weeks 1–4** (more than that tends to fail for beginners):
1. Hit your protein target (185g) every day — this matters more than perfect calorie precision early on
2. Log every workout (weight/reps/RPE) so Phase 2 progression is based on real data, not memory
