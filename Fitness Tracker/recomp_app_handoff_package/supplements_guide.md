# Supplement Guide

> Only supplements with reasonably strong or at least honestly-labeled evidence are included. Nothing here is required — food and training do almost all of the work. These are conveniences and small edges, not shortcuts.

---

## Creatine Monohydrate — **Recommended (strong evidence)**
- **Purpose:** Increases muscle phosphocreatine stores, supporting strength/power output and, over time, lean mass gain
- **Benefits:** One of the most studied supplements in sports nutrition; consistent small-to-moderate benefit for strength, power, and training volume tolerance
- **Risks:** Very safe in healthy adults. Minor initial water retention (often 1-2 lb) is common and not fat gain. If you have any kidney condition, check with a doctor first.
- **Timing:** Doesn't matter much — daily consistency is what counts, not pre/post-workout timing
- **Dosage:** 3-5g/day, ongoing. No loading phase needed.
- **Interactions:** None significant. Just make sure you're drinking enough water, especially given your soccer sweat losses.

## Whey Protein (or any protein powder) — **Recommended for convenience (not essential)**
- **Purpose:** An easy way to hit your 185g/day protein target without needing 6 chicken breasts
- **Benefits:** Fast, convenient, well-absorbed; identical muscle-building value to food protein once total daily intake is matched
- **Risks:** Some people get GI discomfort from whey concentrate (usually lactose) — whey isolate or a plant-based blend fixes this if it happens
- **Timing:** Whenever it's convenient — the "30-minute anabolic window" idea is outdated; total daily protein matters far more than timing
- **Dosage:** 20-40g per serving as needed to close the gap between food and your target
- **Interactions:** None significant

## Vitamin D — **Conditionally recommended (get tested first)**
- **Purpose:** Bone health, immune function, and muscle function; deficiency is common in people with a desk job and limited sun exposure
- **Benefits:** Corrects deficiency if present — meaningful if you're actually low, negligible if you're not
- **Risks:** It's fat-soluble and accumulates in the body, so more is not better — high-dose supplementing without knowing your levels can cause harm
- **Timing:** With a meal containing some fat, for absorption
- **Dosage:** A blood test (25-OH vitamin D) is the right first step; if deficient, a doctor can recommend an appropriate dose (commonly 1,000-2,000 IU/day for maintenance, higher short-term to correct a real deficiency)
- **Interactions:** Minimal at normal doses

## Fish Oil / Omega-3 — **Optional**
- **Purpose:** General anti-inflammatory support and cardiovascular health
- **Benefits:** Reasonably good evidence for cardiovascular markers; evidence for directly speeding tendon healing specifically is emerging/mixed, not strong — don't rely on it for the knee, the loading protocol is what's doing that work
- **Risks:** Fishy aftertaste/burps at high doses; mild blood-thinning effect — caution if you're on blood thinners
- **Timing:** With meals
- **Dosage:** ~1-2g combined EPA+DHA per day is a common general-health target
- **Interactions:** Blood-thinning medications

## Caffeine — **Optional performance aid**
- **Purpose:** Pre-workout alertness and performance
- **Benefits:** Well-established modest boost to strength/endurance performance
- **Risks:** Given your fixed midnight bedtime, this is the one to actually manage — caffeine has a long half-life, so anything after early-to-mid afternoon can measurably hurt your sleep even if you don't notice it. Cut off caffeine by roughly early-to-mid afternoon (aim for at least 8-10 hours before bed).
- **Timing:** 30-60 minutes pre-workout if training earlier in the day
- **Dosage:** Ordinary coffee-level doses (100-200mg, roughly 1-2 cups) get most of the benefit — no need to chase the higher doses sometimes cited in research
- **Interactions:** Don't stack with other caffeinated pre-workout products without accounting for total intake

## Electrolytes (sodium, potassium, magnesium) — **Recommended around soccer sessions specifically**
- **Purpose:** Replace what you lose through sweat, particularly relevant given regular soccer play
- **Benefits:** Helps prevent cramping and supports hydration/performance in sweaty sessions
- **Risks:** Minimal for healthy individuals; if you have hypertension or a kidney condition, check with a doctor before adding extra sodium
- **Timing:** During or after soccer matches and any hot/heavy-sweat training days
- **Dosage:** Varies by product — a general target is roughly 300-700mg sodium per liter of fluid during heavy sweat sessions
- **Interactions:** None significant for healthy individuals

---

## What's Deliberately Not Here
No fat burners, no pre-workout stimulant "blends" beyond plain caffeine, no unproven joint/tendon supplements, no anything promising fast results. If something isn't listed, it's because the evidence doesn't support recommending it — not an oversight.
