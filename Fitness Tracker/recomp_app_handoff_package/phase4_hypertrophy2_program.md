# Phase 4: Hypertrophy Phase 2 (Weeks 21–32)

> The highest-volume block of the year. Builds on Phase 3's strength base by adding back rep-range hypertrophy work at heavier absolute loads than Phase 2, since you're now considerably stronger. Coaching cues/mistakes/video keywords match earlier phases unless noted.

---

## Phase Goals
- Maximum sustainable training volume for the year — this is the block where most of your muscle growth for the year should accumulate, now that you have a real strength base underneath it
- Return to 8-15 rep ranges on main lifts (heavier weight than Phase 2's equivalent reps, since your strength has moved up through Phase 3)
- **This phase spans the 6-month mark of the program (~Week 26).** Use the Week 24 or Week 28 assessment as a deliberate checkpoint: review 6 months of weight/measurement/photo data and decide whether pure recomp is still working, or whether a short, deliberate lean-bulk or mini-cut block makes more sense for the back half of the year. Neither answer is wrong — it depends what the data shows.
- Continue the tendon protocol unchanged.
- Deload + assessment checkpoints at **Weeks 24, 28, and 32** (three this phase, since it's 12 weeks long).

## Progression Rule (Weeks 21–32)
- **Week 21:** Return to 8-12 rep main lifts at RPE 7, using loads that reflect your Phase 3 strength gains (this will be noticeably heavier than Phase 2's numbers for the same rep range).
- **Weeks 22-23:** Standard progression — add ~5% when top of rep range is hit at RPE ≤7.
- **Week 24 — Deload + Assessment #6 (6-month checkpoint):** Full review. This is the most important assessment of the year so far — weigh the full 6-month trend, not just the last month.
- **Weeks 25-27:** Resume progression from Week 23 numbers.
- **Week 28 — Deload + Assessment #7:** Standard deload/check structure.
- **Weeks 29-31:** Continue progression; by Week 31 you should be pushing RPE 8 on main lifts as the phase peaks.
- **Week 32 — Deload + Assessment #8:** This checkpoint also decides readiness for Specialization (Phase 5), which is the earliest point dynamic/deeper-ROM lower body work gets reintroduced if the knee has been consistently clean.

**New exercise detail — Cable/Band Pull-Through:**
- **Target muscles:** Glutes, hamstrings (hip hinge, very low knee stress — a good volume add for this high-volume phase)
- **Common mistakes:** Squatting the movement instead of hinging, rounding the back
- **Coaching cues:** Push hips back, "hike" the rope/band between your legs, drive hips forward to stand
- **Video search keywords:** "cable pull through exercise form"
- **Beginner modification:** Lighter band tension, reduced range
- **Advanced progression:** Heavier band/cable stack, pause at hip extension

**Cool-down:** same as previous phases

**New exercise detail — Walking Lunge (gated):**
- **Only introduce this if the Week 20 or later assessment showed consistently clean knee status.** If not yet cleared, substitute an additional set of Step-Ups instead.
- **Target muscles:** Quads, glutes, balance/stability
- **Common mistakes:** Overly long stride, front knee traveling far past toes, torso leaning forward
- **Coaching cues:** Shorter stride than feels natural at first; drop straight down, not forward; front shin close to vertical
- **Video search keywords:** "walking lunge proper form knee safe"
- **Beginner modification:** Stationary reverse lunge instead of walking, shorter range
- **Advanced progression:** Add dumbbells, increase stride length gradually

- **Target muscles:** Chest, core (anti-rotation)

**New exercise detail — Reverse Flye (band or dumbbell):**
- **Target muscles:** Rear delts, upper back (shoulder health — helps offset all the pressing volume in this phase)
- **Common mistakes:** Using momentum, shrugging instead of squeezing shoulder blades
- **Coaching cues:** Slight bend in elbows, lead with the pinky/back of hand, squeeze shoulder blades together at the top
- **Video search keywords:** "reverse flye rear delt form"
- **Beginner modification:** Lighter band/dumbbells, seated version
- **Advanced progression:** Slower eccentric, pause at top

## Nutrition Update — 6-Month Checkpoint

This is the natural point to seriously evaluate whether pure recomp is still the right call:

- **If weight is trending down slowly, measurements shrinking, and strength still climbing:** recomp is working — no change needed, just keep going.
- **If weight is flat, measurements are flat, and strength has stalled for 4+ weeks despite good adherence:** this usually means the deficit has quietly become too small to drive fat loss, or too large to support continued muscle gain — worth a deliberate choice between a short (4-8 week) more aggressive cut or a short lean-bulk block, rather than staying in an ambiguous middle ground indefinitely.
- **If strength keeps climbing and body composition keeps improving:** stay the course into Phase 5.

There's no need to decide this in isolation — it's worth weighing against how you feel, not just the numbers.
