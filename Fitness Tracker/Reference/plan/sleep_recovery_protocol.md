# Sleep & Recovery Protocol

---

## Your Schedule
Bedtime ~12:00 AM, wake ~7:00 AM — 7 hours. That's on the lower edge of the commonly recommended 7-9 hours for adults, but it's fixed and consistent, which itself has real value for circadian rhythm and recovery. If flexibility ever opens up to extend toward 7.5-8 hours, that's worth trying — but it's not the priority right now, and there's no need to feel behind about it.

## Wind-Down Routine (last 30-60 minutes before bed)
- Dim lights in the last hour — this signals your body to start producing melatonin
- If you're on a screen, use night mode / warm color temperature
- No heavy meals or caffeine in the hours leading up to bed (see the caffeine cutoff in the supplement guide)
- Light stretching or a few minutes of slow breathing
- Same sequence every night if possible (brush teeth → stretch → lights down → bed) — the repetition itself becomes a cue that speeds up how quickly you fall asleep

## Morning Routine
- Same wake time daily, including weekends where possible — this is the single highest-leverage thing for circadian consistency, more so than bedtime
- Get natural light exposure soon after waking (even a few minutes outside or near a window) — this is one of the most well-supported ways to anchor your body clock
- Hydrate first thing
- Avoid repeatedly hitting snooze — fragmented extra sleep in 9-minute chunks is lower quality than just getting up

## Sleep Hygiene Checklist
- Cool, dark, quiet room
- Consistent sleep/wake time, 7 days a week
- No caffeine after early-to-mid afternoon
- Limit alcohol, especially close to bedtime — it fragments sleep even though it can help you fall asleep faster
- Reserve the bed for sleep (not scrolling, working, etc.) if you can manage it
- Morning light exposure
- Heavy meals finished a couple hours before bed

## Recovery Scoring (for the app)
A simple composite score, 0-100, built from:
- Sleep hours vs. your 7-hour target (normalized)
- Sleep quality (self-rated 1-5, if you log it)
- Soreness (inverse — higher soreness lowers the score)
- Resting heart rate relative to your personal baseline, if available from Apple Watch (elevated RHR vs. baseline is a decent proxy for incomplete recovery)
- Mood (1-5)

This doesn't need to be complicated — a simple weighted average is more useful than a falsely precise formula. The value is in the *trend* over days/weeks, not any single day's number.

## Sleep Needs by Training Intensity
- **Heavy lifting days or soccer match days:** hitting your full 7 hours matters more than usual — this is when a short 20-minute nap (if your schedule allows) is worth using if the night before was short
- **Deload weeks:** a good time to let any accumulated sleep debt catch up, since training demand is lower
- **Two hard days back-to-back (e.g., heavy Lower day + soccer match within 24 hours):** treat sleep as non-negotiable that night — it's doing more for the knee and everything else than almost anything else you could add
