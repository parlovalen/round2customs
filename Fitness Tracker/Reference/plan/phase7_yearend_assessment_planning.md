# Weeks 49–52: Deload, Full-Year Assessment & Year 2 Planning

---

## Week 49 — Deload
Standard deload structure: cut sets ~40-50% across all four workouts, keep load moderate, RPE 5-6. Nothing new introduced. This is about entering the final stretch fresh, not fatigued.

## Week 50 — Active Recovery / Exploration
Lighter still than Week 49. Keep the 4 sessions but treat them as low-stakes: try an exercise variation you haven't done all year out of curiosity (different grip, different implement, whatever sounds fun), keep loads light, RPE 5-6 throughout. Soccer and daily movement continue as normal. This week exists partly for physical recovery and partly to give you a low-pressure week to reflect on what you've enjoyed most before planning Year 2.

## Week 51 — Full-Year Assessment

This is the big one. Set aside real time for it, not a rushed 10 minutes.

**Compare Week 1 vs. Week 51:**
- Body weight, waist/hip/chest/arm/thigh measurements
- Progress photos side by side
- Strength: compare working weights at matched rep ranges across all main lifts (RDL, Squat, Bench, OHP) — you don't need to test a true 1-rep max; comparing your current top-set weight at 5-6 reps to where you started is more useful and far lower risk
- Knee status: has patellar tendon pain trended down, stayed flat, or worsened over the full year? If it's still a live issue after a full year of the isometric/HSR protocol, this is worth a proper physical therapy or sports medicine evaluation rather than continuing to self-manage
- Sleep consistency: how close did you stay to your target window across the year?
- Habits: which of the habits you built (protein target, workout logging, and whichever you added later) actually stuck without effort by now? Which never took?
- Soccer: has training noticeably affected on-field performance, recovery between matches, or injury frequency (hopefully for the better)?

**Weekly Check-in and Monthly Assessment data** you've logged all year (in the tracker) is the raw material for this — pull the trends rather than relying on memory.

## Week 52 — Year 2 Planning

The beginner "newbie gains" window (where simultaneous muscle gain + fat loss is realistic) typically closes somewhere in the 6-12 month range. By now you're likely past or near the end of it, which changes the optimal strategy for Year 2.

**Decision framework based on your Week 51 assessment:**
- **If body fat is still meaningfully above where you want it (still noticeably above ~15% for a male target range) and strength is still climbing well:** a dedicated cutting block (moderate deficit, ~500 kcal/day, 8-12 weeks) makes sense before shifting to a more deliberate lean bulk.
- **If body fat is close to where you want it and strength gains have started slowing:** a dedicated lean bulk (moderate surplus, ~200-300 kcal/day surplus) for 4-6 months, followed by a shorter cut, is the more standard trained-lifter approach from here.
- **If recomp is still visibly working (rare but possible, especially if some detraining occurred earlier in life that's still being "recovered"):** there's no rule against continuing it — data beats convention.

**Structural notes for Year 2** (not built out in detail yet, but worth knowing):
- Periodization typically gets more deliberate — longer, more focused blocks (a dedicated hypertrophy block, a dedicated strength block) rather than the broader beginner-friendly structure used this year
- Deload frequency may shift to every 5th-6th week instead of every 4th, since trained lifters generally need slightly less frequent deloading than beginners — but this should be based on how your body actually responded to the every-4th-week pattern this year, not assumed
- If the knee has been fully resolved for several months, more dynamic/plyometric soccer-specific work can be integrated more aggressively
- Nutrition macros will need recalculating against your new bodyweight and the chosen goal (cut vs. bulk) — same Mifflin-St Jeor approach, updated inputs
