# Cardio & Mobility

---

## Cardio

**You don't need dedicated cardio days.** Soccer 2-3x/week already provides both aerobic base work and repeated high-intensity efforts (sprints, direction changes) — the combination a structured Zone 2 + HIIT split would otherwise be trying to build. Adding dedicated cardio on top would mostly just add fatigue that competes with your lifting recovery.

What's still worth tracking:

| Metric | Target | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Daily steps | Baseline + gradual increase (see habit progression, Month 3) | General activity/NEAT matters for recomp independent of formal exercise |
| Walking | No formal target beyond steps — incidental walking counts | Low-impact, knee-friendly, free recovery |
| Heart rate zones (if using Apple Watch during soccer) | Zone 2: ~60-70% max HR · Zone 4-5 (sprints/HIIT-equivalent): ~85%+ max HR | Useful context for understanding what soccer is already providing — roughly estimate max HR as 220 minus age (198 for you), used loosely, not as a precise ceiling |
| Recovery cardio (optional) | A 10-15 min easy walk or bike the day after a hard soccer match or heavy Lower day | Promotes blood flow without adding load — genuinely optional, not required |

**If soccer ever drops off** (injury, season break, schedule change) — that's the point to add 1-2 short Zone 2 sessions (20-30 min easy bike/walk) per week to maintain the conditioning base soccer was providing. Not needed while soccer continues at its current frequency.

## Mobility

### Daily Routine (5-10 minutes, separate from workout warm-ups)
Do this every day, not just training days — it's the thing most likely to get skipped once the "big" habits (workouts, nutrition) are established, which is exactly why it's introduced as its own habit in Month 5.

1. **Ankle dorsiflexion rocks** — 10-15 per side (directly supports squat depth and knee mechanics)
2. **90/90 hip switches** — 8 per side
3. **Cat-cow (thoracic spine)** — 8-10 reps
4. **Shoulder dislocates (band or towel)** — 10 reps
5. **World's greatest stretch** (lunge + rotation) — 5 per side

### Weekly Flexibility Work (once per week, 15-20 minutes, separate session)
A slower, more static-stretch-focused session:
- Hip flexor stretch — 45s/side
- Hamstring stretch (standing or seated) — 45s/side
- Quad stretch — 45s/side
- Chest/doorway stretch — 45s/side
- Lat stretch — 45s/side
- Thoracic spine foam rolling — 2 min

### Joint-Specific Health Work

**Shoulder mobility:** band pull-aparts and shoulder dislocates (already in Upper day warm-ups) — do an extra set on non-training days if shoulders feel tight from desk work.

**Hip mobility:** 90/90 switches and hip flexor stretches (desk job = shortened hip flexors — this one matters more for you than most people).

**Ankle mobility:** dorsiflexion rocks — this is the one most directly tied to your knee situation. Restricted ankle mobility forces the knee to compensate during squatting movements, so this isn't just a "nice to have."

**Thoracic spine mobility:** cat-cow and thoracic rotations — especially relevant given a desk job; a stiff upper back changes how the shoulders move under a bar.
