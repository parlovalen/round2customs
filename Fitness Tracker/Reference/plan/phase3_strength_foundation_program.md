# Phase 3: Strength Foundation (Weeks 13–20)

> Builds on Phase 2. Coaching cues, mistakes, and video keywords for carried-over exercises match earlier phases unless noted.

---

## Phase Goals
- Shift from higher-rep hypertrophy work toward heavier loads and lower reps (5-8 range) on main lifts now that technique is well-grooved
- Accessory work stays in the 8-15 range to keep supporting muscle growth while main lifts drive strength
- **Gate check reminder:** by now you should have had two assessment checkpoints (Week 8, Week 12). If knee status has been clean, this is a reasonable phase to begin very gradually increasing squat depth toward full range — still governed by the same rule: no pain increase during or the next morning, sustained for 2+ weeks, before progressing further.
- Continue the tendon isometric protocol unchanged — it doesn't taper as loads increase, if anything it matters more.
- Deload + assessment checkpoints at **Week 16** and **Week 20**.

## Progression Rule (Weeks 13–20)
- **Week 13:** Introduce lower rep ranges (5-8) on main lifts at a load that feels like RPE 7 for that rep range — this will feel heavier than Phase 2's top-end weights even though it's a similar percentage of your capacity, because fewer reps means less fatigue accumulation. Don't rush the jump; let RPE be your guide, not ego.
- **Week 14:** If Week 13 hit the top of the rep range at RPE ≤7, add ~5% load.
- **Week 15:** Repeat progression. RPE 7-8.
- **Week 16 — Deload + Assessment #4 (1-month mark of this phase):** Cut sets ~40%, reduce load ~10-15%, RPE 5-6. Full photo/measurement/strength review + knee check.
- **Week 17:** Resume from Week 15 numbers, add ~5%.
- **Week 18-19:** Continue standard progression, RPE up to 8 by Week 19 as the phase builds toward its heaviest work.
- **Week 20 — Deload + Assessment #5:** Same structure. This checkpoint also decides whether you're ready for Hypertrophy Phase 2's higher volume demands, or whether an extra stabilization week is warranted first (that's a legitimate, healthy option if recovery markers have been shaky).

## A Note on Heavier Loading and the Knee

Lower-rep, heavier work generally means fewer total repetitions under load per session, which is often *better* tolerated by a tendon in recovery than high-volume, high-rep work — but the loads per rep are higher. If you notice the box squat or front squat starting to bother the knee as loads climb, the right move is to hold load steady and let reps/sets be the progression variable for a week or two, not to push through. This is exactly what the Week 16/20 checkpoints are for.

## Nutrition Update
Same 2-week reassessment rule. By now you'll have 3+ months of weight/measurement data — if recomp has clearly stalled (no change in either direction for 4+ consecutive weeks despite good adherence), that's worth a conversation about whether a deliberate mini-cut or lean-bulk block makes more sense than continuing pure recomp. We don't need to decide that now — the Week 20 or later Week 32 checkpoint is a natural place to revisit it.
